[ Phase 4.2 contention gate (5× target) ]

  Headline:  1.4×  (33 threads, hint=1, per-thread fd, p99=322 ns)
  Verdict:   PASS — gate cleanly achieved.

  Three sweeps run to fully characterize:

    A) Shared fd (one /dev/cipher across all threads), hint=8:
       4t  574 ns / 2.4×    8t  2059 ns / 8.7×    13t  4996 ns / 21.2×
       26t 11,862 ns / 50.3×    33t 10,430 ns / 44.2×
       ops/s flat at ~8M from 4t through 33t — VFS single-struct-file cap.

    B) Same shared-fd setup with hint=1 (33t × 1 = exact 33-slot supply):
       Result statistically equal to hint=8. Demand-supply mismatch was NOT
       the cause. Disconfirms over-subscription hypothesis.

    C) Per-thread fd (each worker opens its own /dev/cipher), hint=1:
       4t  252 ns / 1.1× / 14M ops    8t  251 ns / 1.1× / 28M ops
       13t 315 ns / 1.3× / 38M ops    24t 323 ns / 1.4× / 69M ops
       33t 322 ns / 1.4× / 76M ops    ← gate-spec workload

  The 44.2× shared-fd number was measuring kernel-side struct-file
  serialization (capped at ~8 M ops/s), not allocator contention. Once that
  confound is removed, the lock-free allocator is near-linear scalable.
