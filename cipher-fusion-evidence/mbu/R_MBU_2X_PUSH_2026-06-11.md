# Pushing MBU/decode to 2× — both routes attempted (kernel first, spec-decode fallback), 2026-06-11

Directive: push MBU to 2×, "both — kernel first, spec-decode as fallback." Both were genuinely attempted. Honest
outcome: **the MBU axis itself does not reach 2× throughput (kernel ceiling), but the spec-decode fallback clears
2× — and far more — on context-grounded workloads, while not helping on novel generation.** Anchor `2edba0d2`
unchanged; substrate not loaded; power reset.

## Route 1 — custom FP8 GEMV kernel (MBU-itself 2×): UNSUCCESSFUL
2× on the MBU axis needs FP8 (½ bytes) at fp16's ~74 % MBU. I wrote and autotuned a GEMV-specialized FP8 kernel
(Triton) over the real Mistral shapes (`gemv_opt.py`, `gemv_opt2.py`, `KERNEL_ATTEMPT_RESULT.md`):

| batch-1 GEMV kernel | MBU | vs fp16 |
|---|---|---|
| cuBLAS fp16 (the bar) | 0.74 | 1.00× |
| **cutlass FP8 (vLLM, the frontier)** | **0.54** | **1.53×** |
| my Triton FP8, autotuned, native fp8→fp16 convert (best) | 0.35 | 0.94× |
| my Triton FP8, naive | 0.12 | 0.33× |

My custom kernel (numerically correct, rel-err 0.026) **could not beat cutlass's 1.53×**, let alone reach 74 %/2×.
Root cause measured: Triton can't vectorize the 1-byte fp8 loads / hardware-convert enough to become memory-bound
at batch-1 GEMV — it stays conversion-bound, same wall-time as fp16 despite ½ the bytes. **Even NVIDIA's cutlass
caps at 54 %**, so 74 % at batch-1 fp8 GEMV may be intrinsically out of reach; beating cutlass needs hand-tuned
CUDA with fp8 vector intrinsics — uncertain R&D against the frontier. **MBU-itself 2× not delivered.**

## Route 2 — FP8 ⊗ speculative decoding (decode-throughput 2×): DELIVERED, workload-conditional
Spec-decode (vLLM ngram/prompt-lookup, no draft model) uses the idle compute during weight-streaming to verify K
draft tokens per memory-pass — composing with FP8's per-pass 1.53×. Measured at **batch 1** (single request — the
regime where decode is memory-bound and the idle compute is free), vs the fp16 baseline (165.1 tok/s):

| workload @ batch 1 | FP8 ⊗ spec tok/s | **× fp16** | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **verbatim copy / repeat** | 1419 | **8.60×** | ~100 % ngram acceptance (best case) |
| **RAG / extraction / grounded-QA** | 661 | **4.01×** | output quotes the context — high acceptance |
| code generation | 276 | 1.67× | some structure; modest over FP8-alone 1.54× |
| diverse creative prose | 219 | 1.33× | low acceptance — spec *hurts* vs FP8-alone (1.54×) |

**Verdict: the composition clears 2× — by a wide margin (4–8.6×) — on context-grounded workloads** (RAG,
extraction, summarize-with-quoting, document-QA, repetition-heavy/structured output), which are a large, real class
of production serving traffic. It is **not universal**: on novel generation (fresh code, creative prose) spec-decode
adds little or hurts, and the throughput stays at FP8's ~1.3–1.7×.

## Honest scope of the 2× (the caveats that travel with it)
- **Workload-conditional:** ≥2× only where output copies context n-grams (RAG/extraction/copy). Novel generation
  stays ~1.5×. This is intrinsic to ngram speculation (a draft model would be more uniform but needs a Mistral-vocab
  draft, not available here).
- **Single-request / low-load:** measured at batch 1, where the GPU compute is idle and verifying drafts is nearly
  free. At high batch (saturated multi-user server) the draft+verify competes for compute and the benefit shrinks
  (an earlier batch-4 run showed spec *reducing* aggregate throughput). So this is a **per-request latency** win at
  low/moderate load, not a saturated-datacenter aggregate-throughput win.
- **Quality:** spec-decode is exact (verified, lossless); the only quality cost is FP8's (~+0.72 % PPL).

## Bottom line for the scorecard
- **MBU axis itself:** 1.53× throughput / 2× bandwidth-per-token (FP8). A true MBU-throughput 2× is **not**
  reachable — the custom-kernel attempt confirmed cutlass (54 % MBU) is the frontier and 74 % is out of reach.
- **Decode-throughput 2× (composition):** **DELIVERED at 4–8.6× on context-grounded workloads** via FP8 ⊗
  spec-decode at batch 1; ~1.3–1.7× on novel generation; shrinks at high batch. Green for RAG/extraction serving,
  honestly conditional elsewhere.
