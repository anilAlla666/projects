## Findings
1. **DVFS alone (lossless) caps at 1.39×.** Power-capping fp16 drops the V/f point: at 250 W the GPU runs 825 MHz,
   throughput falls only to 1969 (−23 %) while power falls to 249 W (−45 %) ⇒ tok/W 1.39×. Below 250 W throughput
   collapses faster than power (200 W → 1.25×), so 250 W is the DVFS sweet spot. This is the substrate's VOLT lever,
   lossless (identical outputs).
2. **4-bit alone gives 1.74×** (less work / Joule: fewer weight bytes streamed, lower-energy GEMMs), at default power.
3. **Composed, the levers stack to ≥2×:** 4-bit @ 300 W = **2.00×**, @250 W = 2.18×, @200 W = 2.42× — monotonic.
   The 2× point still delivers **3373 tok/s, 32 % ABOVE the fp16 baseline's 2550**, so the efficiency gain is free
   of throughput cost (it is a throughput gain *and* an efficiency gain). Even at 200 W (2.42×), throughput (2723)
   still beats the fp16/700 W baseline.
4. **Honest split:** of the 2.00×, the lossless DVFS half is ~1.39× and the remainder rides on 4-bit (quality-costed).
   A *lossless* 2× is NOT available here (DVFS-only ceiling 1.39×); the 2× target is met only by accepting 4-bit.

## Moat / scorecard framing
- **DVFS (VOLT) is a substrate actuator** — the 1.39× lossless efficiency is a real substrate-delivered lever.
- **4-bit (machete) is hosted** — not substrate-exclusive (see MBU report: machete ≈ marlin), but the substrate
  *does host it*, and the platform composes VOLT⊕machete into the 2× result.
- So **TPW = 2× is GREEN as a platform deliverable** (substrate VOLT + hosted 4-bit), with the caveat that ~half
  the gain is quality-costed quantization, not lossless DVFS. If the requirement is *lossless* TPW, the honest
  number is **1.39×**; with 4-bit accepted, **2.0–2.4×**.
